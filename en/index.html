The experiments that shaped physics

The history of physics through its turning points: the context, the people behind them, and a simulation that lets you run each experiment yourself.

Arc 1 · The quantum crisis (1900–1927)

  1. 01Blackbody radiation1859–1900Why hot objects glow, and how a formula from 1900 planted the first seed of quantum physics. With a blackbody simulation you can run yourself.
  2. 02The photoelectric effect1887–1916Why bright red light cannot knock out a single electron, and how Einstein explained it in 1905. With a simulation you can run yourself.
  3. 03Rutherford scattering1909–1913Alpha particles bouncing back from gold foil, and how Rutherford concluded that the atom is almost empty. With a simulation and the original 1913 data.
  4. 04The Bohr model and hydrogen lines1885–1913Why hot hydrogen glows in a few sharp colours, and how Bohr explained them in 1913. With a simulation where you make the electron jump.

  6. 06de Broglie and matter waves1923–1924Why an electron in an atom can only sit on a few orbits once it is also a wave. With a simulation of an electron wave wrapped around a Bohr orbit.
  7. 07The Schrödinger equation1925–1926Why a wave equation picks out the energy levels of atoms by itself, and how an electron gets through a wall it cannot climb. With a simulation that lets you solve the equation.
  8. 08Heisenberg's uncertainty principle1927Why the position and velocity of an electron cannot both be known to arbitrary precision, and the real story behind 1927. With simulations of a wave packet and the gamma-ray microscope.
  9. 09Davisson–Germer: electrons interfere1925–1927An exploding flask of liquid air, a damaged nickel target and the first evidence that electrons are waves. With simulations of the 1927 experiment and electrons one at a time through two slits.
  10. 10Solvay 1927: Bohr vs Einstein1927Brussels, October 1927: Einstein challenges Bohr with thought experiments, and his question waits nearly forty years for an answer. With a simulation of Einstein's recoiling slit.
